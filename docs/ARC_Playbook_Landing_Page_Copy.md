## 2. The problem

**Kicker** The gap

**H2** Shipping code is not the same as shipping a product.

**Lead**
AI closed the distance between an idea and a working interface. It did not
answer any of the questions that decide whether the thing survives contact with
a real user.

**Body**
AI can generate an interface. It can write code. It can connect APIs. It can
deploy an application.

None of those things automatically make what you built a product.

**The unanswered questions** (rendered as a grid)
Who is this for? · What problem are you solving? · Will anyone pay for it? ·
What belongs in the MVP? · How should it be architected? · What data does it
need? · What can go wrong? · How will you evaluate the AI? · How will you
secure it? · What should you measure? · How do you launch it?

**Takeaway**
The Playbook is the sequence for answering those in an order that does not
waste the work you already did.

## 4. The five-step ARC system

**Kicker** The system

**H2** Five steps, in the order that makes each one cheaper.

**Lead**
Every step is cheaper than the one after it. That is the whole argument for
doing them in this order.

1. **Validate** — Is this worth building at all?
   Turn the idea into a hypothesis with a named customer, a named problem and
   the evidence that would change your mind.
2. **Define** — What exactly are we building, and what are we not?
   Value proposition, the one workflow that matters, the requirements, and an
   explicit list of what the MVP excludes.
3. **Architect** — How will this hold up once it is real?
   Application shape, AI components, data, integrations, infrastructure and the
   build sequence that follows from them.
4. **Harden** — How do we know it works, and what happens when it does not?
   Evaluation criteria for the AI itself, then security, privacy, monitoring
   and the operational load you are taking on.
5. **Launch** — How does anyone find it, and how do we know it worked?
   Distribution, pricing, the measures that matter, and how you iterate once
   real users arrive.

## 5. Preview

**Kicker** From inside

**H2** Four pages, before you pay for the rest.

**Lead**
If these are not useful on their own, the other two hundred pages will not save
it.

**From module 02 · Validation — Set the go / no-go threshold before you look**
Write the number that means stop before you run the test. A threshold chosen
afterwards is a description of what you already wanted to do.

**From module 04 · Architecture — Mark the AI boundary on the diagram**
Everything inside the boundary can be wrong in ways you cannot fully prevent.
Everything outside it is expected to be deterministic. Teams that skip this
line end up defending model output as if it were a database read.

**From module 06 · Evaluation — An eval set made of real inputs, not clean ones**
Build it from the messiest twenty inputs you can find. If you cannot find
twenty real inputs, you have a validation problem rather than an evaluation
problem.

**From module 07 · Production readiness — The alert that actually pages someone**
List every signal you plan to watch, then cross out the ones nobody would get
out of bed for. What remains is your monitoring. The rest is a dashboard.

## 8. FAQ

**What exactly do I get, and in what format?**
A PDF of the Playbook plus a ZIP of editable Markdown: the worksheets, the 20
ARC Skills, the 20 reference architectures and the 16 workflow templates. No
login, no application to learn, no subscription.

**Is this a course?**
No. There are no videos and no certificate. It is a working document you fill
in while you build, which is why the worksheets ship as editable Markdown as
well as print pages.

**The three libraries are open source. Why are they in a paid bundle?**
Because they are, and we say so on this page. The ARC Skills, reference
architectures and workflow templates are MIT licensed and free on GitHub. What
you are paying for is the Playbook and the curation: each library is cut down,
ordered to the five-step system, and attached to the module where you would
actually reach for it. If you only want the raw catalogs, take them from
GitHub.

**Will it be out of date in six months?**
The model names will be. The sequence will not. The Playbook deliberately
avoids naming specific models and vendors in the method itself, and you get
free updates to this edition.

**Do I need an account?**
No. You buy it, we email you a private download link, and the link works on
more than one device. There is nothing to log into.

**Can I use this with my team?**
Yes, for one team. If you want it as internal training material across a
larger organisation, email us and we will sort out a licence rather than have
you buy twelve copies.

**What if it is not useful?**
Email us within 30 days and we refund it. We would rather return $39 than have
a product with a reputation for wasting people's time.

**Do you also do this work for people?**
Yes. The Playbook is the method; ARC Advisory is the method applied to your
specific product by the people who wrote it.
